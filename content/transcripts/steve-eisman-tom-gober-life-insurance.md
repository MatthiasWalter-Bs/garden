## Key Topics Covered

1. **The shift from mutual to for-profit life insurance companies**
2. **The rise of private equity ownership (Apollo, KKR, Brookfield)**
3. **"Captive reinsurance" schemes that allow companies to hide leverage**
4. **Offshore subsidiaries (Bermuda, Barbados, Cayman Islands)**
5. **GAAP vs statutory accounting differences**
6. **Underfunded liabilities ($7B vs $200M real assets example)**
7. **Duration mismatches (borrowing short, investing long)**
8. **Questionable ratings from smaller agencies (Egan Jones)**

## Eisman's Conclusion

This is a slow-boiling crisis. Private equity has taken over life insurance, increased risk through affiliated investments, hidden leverage through offshore captive reinsurers, and regulators are not equipped to handle it. Nothing has blown up yet because of the 10+ year bull market in credit — but when something bad happens, the industry is way overlevered.
